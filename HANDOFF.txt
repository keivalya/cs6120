0. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH SUMMARY
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The core science is real and interesting: on the scene-fixed LIBERO-Goal suite,
language is causally necessary and scale-invariant (RQ1), and there is an
asymmetric grounding gap — models bind object NOUNS strictly but treat action
VERBS as near-optional (OpenVLA-OFT keeps ~97.5% TSR under wrong_action) (RQ2).
RQ3 (kinematic divergence) is real but small-n. The prior draft's RQ4/RQ5/RQ6 were
FABRICATED from hardcoded arrays and were removed; RQ4 was then re-implemented for
real, but the shipped RQ4 CSV still covers only 2 of 3 models (1.3). The paper is
scoped to RQ1-RQ4, with RQ5 deferred by decision.

1.6  "Scene is provably fixed across conditions."  (FOUND 2026-07-31)
It is not, and report/scene_fixed_check.json has said so — "pass": false — while
paper_acl.tex asserted the opposite. Three separate things hid it: the check wrote
ONE shared file that each model's aggregate run overwrote (so the committed copy
described only whichever model ran last), it keyed on LINE INDEX instead of the
recorded episode, and its result never affected any exit code.
ROOT CAUSE, and it is not a runner bug: the initial scene is NOT a pure function of
(task, episode). The runners call env.seed(...) once when the env is built, and
every reset advances that RNG, which perturbs object poses even though init_state
pins qpos/qvel. So the scene an episode lands on ALSO depends on how many resets
its OS process has already served. Any cell finished by a second process sits on a
different scene than its siblings, and the output looks perfectly normal.
MEASURED (episodes whose scene differs across conditions):
    smolvla      seed7 task0 only (8/10); every single-process task is clean
    openvla      EVERY task, both seeds (conditions split Jul-10 / Jul-15)
    openvla_oft  EVERY task on seed7 (`original` alone was re-run Jul-25)
The clincher is smolvla task0: a job hit the wall after `nonsense` ep8, and ep9 —
written by the resuming process — is on a different scene than ep0-8 of the SAME
condition, one episode apart.
FIXED IN CODE (commit 953b796): run/scene_atomic.py makes the TASK the unit of
atomicity — one process produces all conditions x all episodes for a task, or the
task's partial output is deleted and redone. aggregate.py now exits 2 on a failing
check and writes report/scene_fixed_check_<model>_<suite>.json per model.
CONSEQUENCE FOR GPU PLANNING. The invariant to protect is "a task is never split
across two PROCESSES", and scene_atomic now enforces that on its own: a task cut
off by the wall is wiped and redone from scratch on the next run, never merged.
So a multi-task job is still CORRECT — the sbatch already starts a fresh process
per task — and job sizing is purely about not wasting GPU time. Size so a task
comfortably finishes: ~3 h per task for smolvla and openvla (7 conditions x 10
episodes), ~8 min for OFT, so OFT can do all 10 tasks in one 2 h job while
openvla wants 1-2 tasks per job. Short --time requests also backfill much better.
What you must NOT do is assume a resumed cell is usable: "just resubmit, it
resumes" is exactly what broke the grid.

1.7  DESIGN RULE THAT FOLLOWS FROM 1.6 — and it drives GPU cost
Any two conditions you intend to COMPARE must be produced by the same process,
because that is the only way they share initial scenes. So the condition list is
not a free parameter you can extend later: adding a condition to a model means
re-running EVERY condition you want it compared against, for every task.
Consequence for the word-class ablation (nouns_masked / verb_dropped, see 1.8):
  * OFT — folded in now. Its 9-condition grid (7 causal + 2 ablation) is ~1.7 h
    per seed at ~6.8 s/episode, so it costs essentially nothing. Jobs 8853355/56.
  * openvla / smolvla — NOT folded in. At ~160 s/episode the 2 extra conditions
    are ~18 GPU-h per model, and the deliberate plan (2026-07-31) is to see the
    OFT result before spending that. The catch: the openvla causal grid running
    NOW is 7-condition, so if the ablation is later added to openvla, that grid
    is discarded and re-run (~62 GPU-h). Decide before openvla gets far.

1.8  THE RQ2 REFRAME  (2026-07-31) — read analyze/instruction_information.py
The "asymmetric grounding gap" (nouns bound strictly, verbs near-optional) is not
a model deficiency; it is the information structure of LIBERO-Goal.
    H(task) = 3.32 bits | H(task | noun set) = 0.00 | H(task | verb set) = 1.55
All 10 noun sets are distinct, while "put" alone covers 6 of 10 tasks. Conditional
on the nouns the verb carries ZERO task information, so ignoring it is OPTIMAL
here. wrong_action cannot detect verb-blindness either: 7 of its 10 substitutions
are put->push, preserving the noun set and hence the task. Only tasks 0/3/7 swap a
true antonym. Split that way, SmolVLA drops 25.3 pp on antonyms vs 10.7 pp on the
rest, while OFT is unmoved by either (12/12 on antonyms — told "turn off the
stove" it turns it on). OFT's verb-blindness is real; the suite-wide claim was not.
wrong_object is three conditions in a trench coat: another task's VERBATIM
instruction (task4 -> task2), a novel coherent goal, and DEGENERATE self-reference
("put the bowl in the bowl") where failure is meaningless. In the decisive case
all three models do the task the new nouns NAME and never the original (smolvla
17/20, openvla 4/4, oft 4/4) — compliance, scored by the paper as a 0% failure and
written up as a "universal performance collapse to 7.1%".
FALSIFIABLE TEST, now running on OFT: verb_dropped ("the bowl on the stove",
0 bits removed) should cost ~nothing; nouns_masked ("put the thing on the thing",
3.32 bits removed) should be catastrophic. If verb_dropped collapses, the account
above is WRONG and the models genuinely need the verb token. Do not write up the
reframe until this returns.

1.11 LITERATURE SURVEY 2026-07-31 — the paper's positioning changed
Five papers now report that VLAs largely IGNORE language, which looks like a direct
contradiction of our RQ1. All verified and cited:
  LIBERO-PRO      2510.03827  Zhou et al.  outputs unchanged under corrupted
                              instructions / messy tokens; 90% -> 0.0% generalised
  Flatness        2606.23641  Zhang & Bisk  names "instruction blindness"; SAM
                              finetuning, >60% improvement in instruction following
  Stable Lang.    2601.04052  Zhan et al.   "modality collapse"; visual features
                  Guidance                  dominate the gradient
  Not All Feat.   2603.19233  Grant et al.  mechanistic; injecting baseline
                              activations into NULL-PROMPT episodes recovers
                              near-identical behaviour (6 models, 394K episodes)
  RADAR           2602.10980  Chen et al.   generalisation benchmark
THE RECONCILIATION, and it is now the paper's opening move: instruction-blindness is
what you measure where the SCENE identifies the task. The LIBERO suites differ
exactly here — Spatial varies positions of one object, Object varies objects in
similar layouts, and GOAL puts heterogeneous goals in a SHARED environment. Only in
Goal can a policy not succeed by recognising the layout. Our suite is the one where
language must matter, and the finding is that even there redundancy has not gone
away, it moved down to the verb.
>>> A SCOOP THAT ISN'T, but nearly was. Zhan et al.'s Residual Affordance Steering
    is, up to normalisation, the RQ5 operator in report/RQ4_RQ5_SPEC.txt:
      pi(a|o,l) ~ exp[s(a|o,EMPTY) + gamma*(s(a|o,l) - s(a|o,EMPTY))]
    and it reports +29.85 points. RQ5 as specified is DONE, by someone else, and it
    works. Do not run it as a contribution. The Conclusion now cites it and makes the
    sharper point instead: on a suite whose verbs are redundant given its nouns, a
    method that raises verb sensitivity is being scored on a probe that cannot
    separate better grounding from a policy pushed OFF the optimal one.
    Checked for a real scoop of the information-theoretic contribution — nearest work
    is mutual-information data curation over states/actions (2502.08623), not
    word-class content of an instruction set. Nothing overlapping found.

5. CLUSTER FACTS THAT MATTER FOR SCHEDULING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  * On paper you have EIGHT concurrent GPUs across three INDEPENDENT QOS:
      gpu              8 h wall   4 running   8 submitted   4 GPUs
      gpu-short        2 h wall   2 running   8 submitted   2 GPUs
      gpu-interactive  2 h wall   2 running   4 submitted
    IN PRACTICE (measured 2026-07-30) that is closer to four. `gpu` outranks the
    other two on the shared nodes, so while `gpu` is saturated — 128 jobs deep —
    jobs on gpu-short/gpu-interactive sit at
      "Nodes required for job are DOWN, DRAINED or reserved for jobs in higher
       priority partitions"
    and never start. Both a gpu-short and a gpu-interactive submission of the 7B
    smoke test were starved this way for over an hour. Plan the real work on `gpu`
    and treat the small QOS as opportunistic only. Short --time requests are the
    one lever that reliably helps: a 25-minute job backfills into gaps that an 8 h
    job cannot.
    `short` is CPU-only, 2-day wall, 50 jobs, and is NOT contended — put env
    builds and ALL aggregation there. Only analyze/attention_extract.py needs a
    GPU outside the runners.
  * GPUs are heavily contended: 128 jobs pending on `gpu` and every GPU on
    d4055/d1026/d1013/d1022/c2204 allocated at the time of writing. Queue time,
    not GPU-hours, is the binding constraint. Short --time requests backfill much
    better than long ones.
  * Typed GRES works and is what selects bf16-capable hardware:
    --gres=gpu:h200:1 (d4055, 8 GPUs) or --gres=gpu:a100:1 (d1026, 3). The two 7B
    runners load bfloat16; on sm_70 V100 that falls back to the math backend and
    is several times slower. SmolVLA (0.45B) is fine on V100 — leave it untyped
    for the widest pool.
  * Keep HF_HOME on /scratch. The home per-user quota (76 GiB) fills from
    ~/.cache/{huggingface,uv,pip}; currently 13 GB used, ~7.4 GB of it ~/.cache.

6. OPEN DECISION THAT BLOCKS RQ5  (unchanged; read report/RQ4_RQ5_SPEC.txt)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The fabricated RQ5 claimed one "logit modulation" operator
    logits' = logits(I) + alpha * (logits(I) - logits(I_blank))
for all three models. That operator is only DEFINED for OpenVLA, which predicts
discretized action tokens. OpenVLA-OFT (use_l1_regression=True) and SmolVLA
(flow-matching) have CONTINUOUS action heads with NO action-token logits. So RQ5
is a method-design task, not a pure run. Either (A) scope RQ5 to OpenVLA and
present the continuous variant as an extension, or (B) define a unified operator
reducing to logit-space for OpenVLA and action-space
    a' = a(I) + alpha*(a(I) - a(I_blank))
for OFT/SmolVLA. Decide BEFORE any RQ5 runs. Currently deferred (section 2).
