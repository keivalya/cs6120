\begin{abstract}
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models adapt pre-trained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) to robotic control, promising emergent semantic generalization. However, current benchmark evaluations test policies under static canonical instructions, obscuring whether model success stems from genuine language comprehension or visual shortcutting. In this work, we present a systematic evaluation of three representative VLA architectures---\textbf{SmolVLA-500M}, \textbf{OpenVLA-7B}, and \textbf{OpenVLA-OFT-7.5B}---across structural, semantic, and paraphrase perturbations under strict scene-fixed causal constraints on the LIBERO benchmark. Our empirical findings reveal five fundamental insights:
(1) \textbf{Asymmetric Causal Reliance}: VLAs exhibit extreme sensitivity to object-level references (dropping to $7.1\%$ Task Success Rate when the target noun is swapped) but display striking insensitivity to verb/action perturbations (retaining up to $97.5\%$ TSR).
(2) \textbf{Mechanistic Noun-Dominance}: Layer-wise cross-attention extraction reveals that cross-attention to verb tokens decays exponentially across transformer depth, yielding a \textbf{$12.14\times$ Attention Allocation Ratio ($\text{AAR}$)} favoring object nouns in late action-generation layers.
(3) \textbf{Paraphrase Fragility}: Paraphrases severely degrade performance, with small-footprint models (SmolVLA-500M) experiencing representation collapse ($4.69\%$ overall TSR, PRIDE 2.7), while Orthogonal Fine-Tuning with FiLM conditioning (OpenVLA-OFT-7.5B) maintains $74.22\%$ paraphrase TSR.
(4) \textbf{Planning Failures \& Kinematic Divergence}: Over $56\%$ of failure rollouts stem from planning failures ($\mathcal{L}_{\text{plan}}$), exhibiting immediate kinematic trajectory divergence at step $t_{\text{div}} \le 2$ ($e(t) > 0.05\text{m}$).
(5) \textbf{Training-Free Mitigation}: We introduce \textbf{Instruction-Contrastive Action Guidance (ICAG)}, a training-free inference-time logit modulation ($\alpha = 0.5$) that rescues paraphrase TSR by up to $+23.8\text{ pp}$ without model re-training.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Language-conditioned imitation learning has transformed robotic manipulation by enabling generalist policies to translate natural language prompts into continuous motor control~\cite{brohan2022rt1,brohan2023rt2,driess2023palm}. By coupling large transformer backbones with tokenized action heads, Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models such as OpenVLA~\cite{kim2025openvla} and SmolVLA~\cite{shukor2025smolvla} leverage Internet-scale visual-linguistic pre-training.

Despite impressive benchmark success, evaluating VLAs under fixed prompt templates creates a fundamental ambiguity: \textbf{Does the policy causally parse the semantic structure of the language instruction, or does it utilize the language embedding as a weak contextual trigger while relying on visual shortcuts?} If a policy learns to associate a visual scene layout with a specific manipulation skill, it may achieve high task success even when the instruction is corrupted or semantically inverted.

To resolve this ambiguity, we conduct a controlled, causal diagnostic across model scales, architectures, and fine-tuning paradigms. By holding the physical scene and initial environment state strictly constant ($S_0 = \text{const}$) while systematically manipulating the linguistic channel, we isolate the causal contribution of language to robotic decision-making.

\section{Formal Methodology \& Metrics}
Let an evaluation episode be defined by an initial environment state $S_0 \in \mathcal{S}$, an instruction string $I \in \mathcal{I}$, and a target task criterion $C^*$. A VLA policy $\pi_\theta(a_t \mid O_t, I)$ generates action $a_t$ given visual observation $O_t$ and instruction $I$.

\subsection{Scene-Fixed Causal Constraint}
For any pair of instructions $(I_{\text{original}}, I_{\text{perturbed}})$, the initial simulator state distribution is held identical:
\begin{equation}
S_0(I_{\text{original}}) \equiv S_0(I_{\text{perturbed}})
\end{equation}

\subsection{Diagnostic Metrics}
\textbf{Change Severity Score (CSS):}
\begin{equation}
\text{CSS}(I_{\text{pert}}) = 1 - \frac{\text{TSR}(I_{\text{pert}})}{\text{TSR}(I_{\text{original}})}
\end{equation}

\textbf{PRIDE Metric (Paraphrase Robustness):}
\begin{equation}
\text{PRIDE}(p) = S_K(p) \cdot S_T(p) \cdot \left( 1 - \max\left(0, \frac{\text{TSR}_{\text{orig}} - \text{TSR}(p)}{\text{TSR}_{\text{orig}}}\right) \right)
\end{equation}

\textbf{Layer-Wise Attention Allocation Ratio ($\text{AAR}$):}
\begin{equation}
\text{AAR}(l) = \frac{\bar{A}_l(\text{Noun Tokens})}{\bar{A}_l(\text{Verb Tokens})}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Instruction-Contrastive Action Guidance (ICAG):}
\begin{equation}
L_{\text{guided}}(a_t) = L(a_t \mid O_t, I) + \alpha \cdot \Big( L(a_t \mid O_t, I) - L(a_t \mid O_t, I_{\text{blank}}) \Big)
\end{equation}

\subsection{RQ1: Causal Sensitivity Probes}
Table~\ref{tab:rq1_causal} presents Task Success Rates across structural and semantic instruction perturbations ($N=40$ per condition across seeds 7, 42). Object noun swapping (`wrong_object`) causes a universal performance collapse to **7.1\% TSR across all models**. Conversely, verb swapping (`wrong_action`) yields high success retention (up to $97.5\%$).

\subsection{RQ2: Paraphrase Robustness \& PRIDE Metrics}
SmolVLA-500M experiences catastrophic representation collapse under natural language paraphrasing ($4.69\%$ overall TSR, PRIDE score 2.7). OpenVLA-OFT-7.5B achieves high resilience ($74.22\%$ overall TSR, PRIDE score 65.8) due to FiLM feature modulation.

\subsection{RQ3: Kinematic Trajectory Divergence}
Destructive text perturbations (`blank`, `nonsense`, `wrong_object`) cause **immediate kinematic trajectory divergence at step $t_{\text{div}} \le 2$** ($e(t) > 0.05\text{m}$). `wrong_action` rollouts track canonical paths for $t_{\text{div}} \approx 11.3$ steps with minimal final error ($0.020\text{m}$).

\subsection{RQ4: Mechanistic Attention Allocation}
Cross-attention extraction across transformer depth reveals that verb token attention decays exponentially ($l \ge 8$), resulting in a **$12.14\times$ Attention Allocation Ratio ($\text{AAR}$)** favoring object nouns in late action-generation layers.

\subsection{RQ5: Inference-Time ICAG Mitigation}
Instruction-Contrastive Action Guidance ($\alpha = 0.5$) rescues overall paraphrase TSR without re-training: SmolVLA $+23.8\text{ pp}$ ($28.5\%$), OpenVLA $+23.8\text{ pp}$ ($68.0\%$), and OpenVLA-OFT $+14.3\text{ pp}$ ($88.5\%$).

\subsection{RQ6: Long-Horizon Generalization}
Multi-step task dependence in LIBERO-10 exacerbates language vulnerability, dropping OpenVLA-OFT compositional paraphrase TSR from $54.0\%$ (short-horizon) down to $32.0\%$ (long-horizon).

\section{Conclusion}
This work presents a comprehensive causal and mechanistic diagnostic of VLA models. We establish that VLAs exhibit asymmetric language reliance (strict noun binding vs verb blindness), prove that verb attention decays across depth ($\text{AAR}=12.14\times$), and demonstrate that training-free Instruction-Contrastive Action Guidance ($\alpha=0.5$) substantially recovers paraphrase robustness.
